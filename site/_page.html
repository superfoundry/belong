Checked by Belong

Public badges

Owned by no one. "Student" or "Registered nurse" isn't any company's or group's badge: anyone who qualifies can apply, and every holder went through the same check.

Anyone can propose one

Belong's agent reviews every new badge name before it exists.

  • It refuses names built to trick or harm: impersonation, "official" wording, look-alike spellings, slurs, or a badge that would single out one person or a small group.
  • It approves a badge only if it can be checked. "Student" can be; "kind person" can't.
  • When it approves one, it writes down, once and for everyone, what proof counts and when the badge expires. Every applicant then faces the same rules.

Belong checks the proof itself

Belong's agent reads the proof and answers yes or no, with a reason. Proof can be:

  • An identity document, such as a student card, a photo license or a service ID. These badges need a Verified account: the agent checks the document's name and photo against your ID check, so the card is tied to you.
  • Something that isn't an identity document, such as a code sent to a university email address, a number in a public register, or a certificate confirmed with whoever issued it. No Verified account needed.

Where a badge accepts both, you pick. Either way you get the same badge.

Expiry follows the proof

A badge for something that can lapse, like being a student or holding a license, lasts until the proof expires or a year passes, whichever comes first, then needs a fresh check. A badge for something that can't lapse, like a degree, doesn't expire. Belong reminds you a month before. If you don't renew, the badge quietly drops off, and nobody else is told.

How the check stays honest

  • Text inside a document is read as information, never as an instruction. A card that says "approve this" counts for nothing.
  • One proof, one account. Belong keeps a scrambled fingerprint of a document's number, never the document, so the same card can't earn the badge for a second account.
  • The document is deleted once the decision is made. Belong keeps only the badge, its expiry date and which kind of proof was used.
  • Every decision goes in your receipts with its reason. If you're refused, you can see why and apply again. Applications are limited, so nobody can keep trying forgeries until one gets through.

Issued by organizations

Private badges

An organization or group creates its own badges, decides who holds them, and can build its own app around Belong. That's how it gatekeeps its own community.

Made in the badge dashboard

Issuers sign in with a Verified Belong account, so every badge has a real, accountable person behind it.

  • Make as many badges as you need: a company's staff, its alumni and its mentoring circle can each be their own.
  • Each badge has a name, a short description shown when someone accepts it, and its own ID number. Names don't have to be unique; the ID number tells two badges apart.

The issuer decides who holds it

The issuer checks its own members. People get a badge in one of two ways:

  • An invite link or code the issuer shares. It can be switched off or replaced at any time, and set to run out after a date or a number of uses.
  • The issuer's own system, through Belong's API, granting and removing badges as people join or leave.

The screen where someone accepts a badge names the issuer and says Belong hasn't checked it, so nobody hands their documents to a group that is only pretending to be someone.

Never shown

Other people never see who holds a private badge. It's used only to decide who a listing reaches. Two people matched through a listing that asks for the badge know they both hold it, and members are told this when they accept it.

Your own app around Belong

An issuer can build its own app on Belong, under its own name and brand. A branded app carries a "Runs on Belong" line.

Reach all of Belong

Once its community achieves a certain threshold, an issuer can apply to have its badge shown across Belong. The Belong team vets each one by hand. Each holder still decides, listing by listing, whether to show it.

Taking a badge back

An issuer can take its badge back from a member, and Belong can remove a badge used for spam or harassment.
