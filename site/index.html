How it works

Nobody browses anyone

No profiles to scroll and no one to message cold. You decide what goes on your listing, and you decide yes or no.

  1. Tell your own AI

    Say what you're looking for or offering, the way you'd say it to a friend. Your AI turns it into a listing: what you want, who you are, and the details that matter. You see every detail and choose which are shown and which are hidden.

  2. Belong compares every listing

    The matcher reads everyone's listings and proposes pairs where each person is what the other asked for, hard limits included. A hidden detail can strengthen a match that already fits. It never makes one on its own.

  3. A match shows only what they chose to show

    Every match is built by the Warden, which checks it against fixed rules. Its reason uses only details you both show, and nothing tells anyone who said no.

  4. You both say yes

    You're introduced only when you both say yes. Until then, neither of you has any way to reach the other.

The standard

A standard, like email

Email lets anyone write to anyone, whatever app they use. Belong does the same for finding each other: public infrastructure, open source and run at cost. The Belong app is one way in, not the product.

Any AI or app

A front end writes listings with you, shows your matches and carries your conversations: the Belong app, an AI assistant you already use, or a client of your own.

Open source

The matcher and the Warden are both published. The Warden still treats the matcher as hostile, so privacy never depends on it behaving well.

Run at cost

Belong exists to make introductions, not to keep your attention. There's nothing to scroll and no feed to keep you on it.

Draft

The standard is a draft. Today the Belong app is its only front end, in a demo. The public API that other AIs and apps will use, and their sign-in, are still being written. Read the draft standard or how to build a front end.

Privacy

What you hide stays between you and Belong

No other person and no front end ever learns what you keep hidden. Belong runs the matcher and can see what it holds, so it keeps as little as it can and publishes the code that handles it.

Shown Shown

  • Seen by the people you match with.
  • The only details a match's reason can use.
  • Checked before your listing goes live: no contact details, nothing that describes one particular person.

Hidden Hidden

  • Never sent to any other person or front end.
  • Never the only reason for a match, and counted only when 20 other people share it.
  • Compared with a match only if you both choose to.
Used for matching, never shown

What Belong keeps

  • Your listing, while it's live. Withdraw it and it's never matched again.
  • Your yes or no, and never tells anyone who said no.
  • A receipt for every check, with no content, in a log that can't be rewritten.
  • Never your messages. Display, memory, notifications and chat are up to the front end you choose.

Sensitive details, like religion, health, politics and income, start hidden, and showing one takes your own confirmation.

Trust and security

Don't trust us. Check.

Every promise names the code that keeps it and the test that proves it.

Tested on every change

Every change runs the full test suite and a simulation of 300 people with an independent leak audit. A single leak fails it, and it can't be merged.

Attacked on purpose

A built-in probe plays a hostile matcher, one attack at a time, and the tests check that the Warden stops every one.

Checks, not judgement

The Warden never decides who matches. It runs fixed rules, with no AI judgement, on everything that goes in and comes out.

Prototype

What's simulated today. The demo runs on synthetic people, and its ID check is simulated. The matcher's AI is reached over OpenAI's API, so it is used only with synthetic and test profiles, and every security check runs as if it were enclosed.
